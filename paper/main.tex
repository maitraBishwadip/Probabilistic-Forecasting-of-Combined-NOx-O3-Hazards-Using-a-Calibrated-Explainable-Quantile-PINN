%------------------------------------------------------------------ ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Bangladesh endures some of the world's highest air-pollution exposures, and the
\emph{joint} upper range of nitrogen oxides (\nox) and ozone (\othree)---chemically
summarised by the total oxidant $\ox=\othree+\notwo$---is disproportionately harmful
to health. Yet no forecast targets this \emph{combined} hazard probabilistically, and
none embeds the governing photochemistry. We present a quantile-regressive
physics-informed neural network (QR-PINN) that emits the \emph{entire} predictive
distribution of a balanced, climatology-referenced combined \nox--\othree\ hazard
index over the next \mbox{24\,h} at hourly resolution, for nine stations during
2014--2016. The forecaster is well calibrated---conformalized quantile regression
attains near-nominal 80/90/95\% coverage that holds across lead time---and transfers
to unseen stations with a small, quantified penalty (leave-one-station-out pinball gap
$+0.025$). We then test, as the central hypothesis, whether embedding \nox--\othree\
chemistry (Leighton photostationary state and \ox\ conservation) improves probabilistic
accuracy. Across three independent experiments it does \emph{not}; explainable-AI
attribution (Integrated Gradients and occlusion, agreement $r=0.917$) shows why: skill
is carried by pollutant-lag and calendar features on an \othree-led target, not by
meteorology. We therefore reposition the physics as a source of \emph{interpretability}
---physically ordered learned rates and recovered per-station emission scales---and
report the negative accuracy result transparently.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Probabilistic forecasting, physics-informed neural networks, quantile regression,
explainable AI, conformal prediction, air quality, ozone, Bangladesh.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

%================================================================== INTRODUCTION
\section{Introduction}
Bangladesh is among the most polluted countries on Earth, with severe health and
economic costs~\cite{worldbank2022}. While particulate matter dominates the winter
inversion season~\cite{billah2026}, the gaseous oxidants \nox\ and \othree\ pose a
distinct, year-round hazard. Crucially, they are not independent: they are locked in
the fast photochemical cycle $\notwo+h\nu\!\rightarrow\!\mathrm{NO}+\othree$,
$\othree+\mathrm{NO}\!\rightarrow\!\notwo$, so that the \emph{total oxidant}
$\ox=\othree+\notwo$ is approximately conserved through titration and is the chemically
meaningful ``combined'' quantity~\cite{seinfeld2016,taheri2024}. Epidemiology shows the
\emph{joint} elevation of \othree\ and \notwo\ (and \ox) is more harmful than either
alone~\cite{fu2022}. The public-health-relevant target is therefore the broad upper
region of a \emph{combined} hazard, not the rare top percentile of a single pollutant.

Operational air-quality prediction is shifting from point estimates to
\emph{predictive distributions}, because exceedance warnings are inherently
probabilistic~\cite{perezvasseur2021}. In parallel, physics-informed neural networks
(PINNs)~\cite{raissi2019,karniadakis2021} embed governing equations as soft training
constraints, and have been applied to air quality---e.g.\ transport-physics graph
networks~\cite{hettige2024} and remote-sensing-constrained chemistry-transport
fusion~\cite{boli2023}. However, these are \emph{deterministic} and
\emph{single-pollutant}; uncertainty-aware PINNs~\cite{yang2021} and quantile-PINNs
exist only outside the atmospheric domain~\cite{hu2025}. No prior work forecasts a
\emph{combined} \nox--\othree\ hazard \emph{probabilistically} with embedded
photochemistry, and none does so for Bangladesh.

We close this gap and, in doing so, ask a sharp scientific question:
\emph{does embedding \nox--\othree\ chemistry actually improve probabilistic forecast
accuracy?} Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{QR-PINN} that outputs the full 24\,h predictive distribution of a
balanced, climatology-referenced combined \nox--\othree\ hazard index---to our knowledge
the first probabilistic, physics-informed, combined-pollutant forecast for Bangladesh.
\item A \textbf{calibrated} forecaster: conformalized quantile regression restores
near-nominal coverage at 80/90/95\% that holds across lead time, with proper-scoring
evaluation, plus quantified \textbf{spatial transfer} (leave-one-station-out, bootstrap CIs).
\item A rigorous, \textbf{honest negative result}: across three independent experiments,
embedded physics is neutral-to-negative for accuracy, and \textbf{explainable AI}
(Integrated Gradients $+$ occlusion, $r=0.917$) explains \emph{why}.
\item A \textbf{reframing} of the physics as interpretability: physically ordered learned
rates (photolysis $\gg$ titration) and recovered per-station emission scales.
\end{itemize}

%================================================================== DATA
\section{Data and Study Area}
We use hourly observations from \textbf{nine} Department of Environment (DoE) stations
across Bangladesh, 2014-01-01 to 2016-12-31 (223{,}776 raw rows). The stations
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a) span a megacity cluster around Dhaka, the port of Chittagong
(Agrabad), and divisional cities, covering markedly different pollution regimes:
traffic/titration-dominated, photochemically \othree-dominated, and jointly
high-burden. The pollutant data are right-skewed and heavily gapped
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}b), and kriged station-mean surfaces (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}c,d)
confirm the expected spatial \emph{anti-structure}---\notwo\ peaks over the urban core while
\othree\ peaks in cleaner peripheral air---the spatial signature of the same titration
chemistry the model must capture.

Each station carries pollutant channels ($\mathrm{NO}$, \notwo, \nox, \othree) plus
reanalysis meteorology and physical drivers (temperature, humidity, wind $u/v$, solar
radiation, boundary-layer height, ventilation coefficient, a photochemical-activity
index, pressure), daily MODIS satellite fields (AOD/NDVI/fire), and calendar terms---26
features in all. Pollutant missingness is heavy (design estimate \othree\ ${\approx}74.7\%$,
\nox\ ${\approx}62.6\%$ \emph{populated}), so we keep per-channel \textbf{missingness
masks} and \textbf{never impute the target}. After quality control and hourly windowing,
the modelling tensor is $9\times 26{,}304\times 26$, giving \textbf{86{,}037 training}
(2014--15) and \textbf{31{,}067 test} (2016) forecast anchors; an anchor requires a full
$L{=}48$\,h input window and a valid future target. Spatial generalisation is assessed by
\textbf{leave-one-station-out} (LOSO).

%================================================================== METHOD
\section{Method: A Quantile Physics-Informed Neural Network}

\subsection{Combined hazard index}
We require a target that is (i)~a graded, continuous hazard suitable for quantile
regression and (ii)~physically and health-meaningful, with neither pollutant dominating
by scale. We adopt a \textbf{rank / quantile-uniform} index
\begin{equation}
H \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,F_{\nox}(\nox) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,F_{\othree}(\othree),
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\bullet}$ is the \emph{training} empirical CDF of each pollutant, so each
marginal is uniform on $[0,1]$. An earlier scale-based index let \othree\ overpower the
combination (training variance share \othree~$72.8\%$ vs.\ \nox~$44.1\%$; prediction
correlation \othree~$0.898$ vs.\ \nox~$0.116$); \eqref{eq:H} rebalances it (variance
share $61.0\%/63.3\%$; prediction correlation \nox~$0.348$/\othree~$0.629$)\footnote{%
\texttt{src: run E5}.}. The \emph{combined extreme} is defined by a climatology-referenced
cutoff,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{thr}&=Q_{30}(H_{\text{train}})=0.3948,\\
p_{t+h}&=\Pr(H_{t+h}\ge \mathrm{thr}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:thr}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the 30th percentile, so $\ge 70\%$ of hours are ``hazardous'' (test base rate
$0.834$)---a deliberate, health-motivated broad class~\cite{fu2022}. The model never
trains on this binary label; the exceedance probability $p_{t+h}$ is read off the
predicted CDF.

\subsection{Architecture and quantile head}
An LSTM encoder (hidden size 64) ingests the 48\,h multivariate window together with a
nine-station embedding (dim.\ 8) and emits a latent state, from which two heads branch
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig2_architecture.png}
\caption{QR-PINN architecture. A shared LSTM encoder feeds a \emph{monotone quantile head}
(the deliverable---non-crossing $Q_\tau(H_{t+h})\!\rightarrow$ predictive CDF/PDF and exceedance
probability) and a \emph{physics head} (a coupled \mbox{NO--\notwo--\othree} box-ODE with the
Leighton and \ox-conservation residuals). Both feed the composite loss; the physics term is a
regulariser/interpretability device, not an accuracy mechanism.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}
The \emph{quantile head} (the deliverable) predicts, for each lead $h\!\in\!\{1,\dots,24\}$
and quantile level $\tau$, a value $Q_\tau(H_{t+h})$ trained with the pinball loss
$\rho_\tau(u)=u\,(\tau-\mathbb{1}\{u<0\})$. To forbid quantile crossing we build the
quantiles monotonically from non-negative increments,
\begin{equation}
Q_{\tau_k}=Q_{\tau_1}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\mathrm{softplus}(\delta_j),\quad \delta_j\in\mathbb{R},
\label{eq:noncross}
\end{equation}
so $Q_{\tau_1}\!\le\!\dots\!\le\!Q_{\tau_K}$ by construction~\cite{cannon2018,koenker1978}.
We train on $\tau\in\{0.05,\dots,0.95\}$ and extend to $\{0.01,\dots,0.99\}$ for tail
calibration. The dense, monotone quantiles define the predictive CDF/PDF per horizon.

\subsection{Embedded physics}
The \emph{physics head} advances per-species concentrations through a coupled box model
at each station. For species $k$ the hourly mass balance is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}C_k}{\mathrm{d}t}={}&\frac{E_k}{H_{\mathrm{mix}}}
-\frac{V_C}{H_{\mathrm{mix}}}C_k-\frac{v_{d,k}}{H_{\mathrm{mix}}}C_k\\
&-\,\Lambda_k P\,C_k+R_k,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
with emission $E_k$, mixing height $H_{\mathrm{mix}}$, ventilation $V_C$, deposition
$v_{d,k}$, wet scavenging $\Lambda_k P$, and chemistry $R_k$. The \nox--\othree\ coupling
enters as two residual penalties: the \textbf{Leighton} photostationary state and
\textbf{\ox\ conservation},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{L}}&=C_{\othree}C_{\mathrm{NO}}-\tfrac{J}{k(T)}\,C_{\notwo},\\
\ox&=\othree+\notwo\quad(\text{slowly varying}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:chem}
\end{equation}
where $J$ scales with the photochemical-activity index and $k(T)$ is Arrhenius in
temperature. All rate parameters are \emph{global and learnable}, making their fitted
values an interpretable scientific output. The full objective combines the
distributional fit with the physics as a soft, annealed regulariser:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}={}&\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{\tau,h}\rho_\tau(H_{t+h},Q_\tau)}_{\text{pinball (primary)}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{box}}\\
&+\lambda_{\mathrm{chem}}(r_{\mathrm{L}}+r_{\ox})+\lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\,\mathcal{R}_{c},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}$ annealed $0\!\rightarrow\!\text{target}$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{data}}$ a concentration data-fit term where observed.

%================================================================== EXPERIMENTS
\section{Experimental Design and Hypotheses}
We score the predictive distribution with strictly proper rules~\cite{gneiting2007}:
average \textbf{pinball} loss and \textbf{CRPS} (from the quantile grid). Calibration uses
prediction-interval coverage (PICP) and width, PIT-based recalibration~\cite{kuleshov2018},
and \textbf{conformalized quantile regression} (CQR)~\cite{romano2019} fit on an early-2016
calibration split and evaluated on late-2016. Hazard skill uses the \textbf{Brier} score on
the exceedance probability $p_{t+h}$ \eqref{eq:thr}. Spatial transfer uses LOSO with a
moving-block bootstrap (block $=24$\,h, $B=1000$) for 95\% CIs. Explainability uses
\textbf{Integrated Gradients}~\cite{sundararajan2017} (completeness-checked, ``missing''
baseline) and model-agnostic \textbf{occlusion}~\cite{fisher2019} in the model's own loss
currency, reported together because saliency on time series is otherwise
unreliable~\cite{ismail2020}. Table~\ref{tab:hyp} states the hypotheses and the experiment
that tests each; all results below are transcribed from the project results log.

%================================================================== RESULTS
\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{The index is genuinely combined (H3)}\label{ssec:combined}
Replacing the scale-based combination with the rank index \eqref{eq:H} raises \nox's
prediction correlation from $0.116$ to $0.348$ and balances the variance shares (\S III-A),
so the forecast responds to \emph{both} pollutants rather than tracking \othree\ alone.

\subsection{A calibrated distributional forecaster (H1)}\label{ssec:calib}
A representative forecast is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:laboni}.
On the original scale-based index, all learned models cut pinball loss by ${\sim}45\%$
versus a climatological quantile baseline ($1.05$ vs.\ $1.93$), confirming genuine skill.
Because the adopted rank index lives on $[0,1]$, absolute values are not comparable across
index definitions, so we report within-index comparisons hereafter. Raw quantiles
\emph{under-cover} (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a; PICP$_{90}=0.839$). PIT recalibration helps but
leaves the upper tail short; \textbf{CQR restores near-nominal coverage at all levels}
(80/90/95\% $\rightarrow$ $0.781/0.892/0.951$; Table~\ref{tab:calib}) at a modest width
cost, and---critically for a 24\,h forecast---this coverage \textbf{holds across lead
time} (CQR PICP$_{90}=0.893/0.887/0.902/0.900$ at $h=1/6/12/24$; Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a inset).
Calibration is the model's strongest, most defensible property.

\subsection{Does the physics help? A rigorous negative result (H2)}\label{ssec:phys}
We tested H2 three independent ways. \emph{(i)}~A physics-weight sweep: pinball is
essentially flat from $\lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}=0$ ($0.2054$) to $0.2$ ($0.2063$) and
\emph{worse} at $0.5$ ($0.2188$), with seed-stable headline numbers ($0.2063$ vs.\
$0.2060$). \emph{(ii)}~Forcing the box-ODE as the sole forecaster was far worse
($+0.1375$ pinball). \emph{(iii)}~A stable, capacity-preserving \emph{hybrid}
(NN residual $+$ semi-implicit box-ODE) is still worse than the free model
(Table~\ref{tab:skill}), \emph{despite being handed observed future meteorology as ODE
forcing}. The verdict is consistent and reported honestly: \textbf{embedding
\nox--\othree\ box chemistry does not improve probabilistic accuracy of $H$} on this
\othree-led target. The engineering was nonetheless fixed---the hybrid ($0.25$) and
physics-only ($0.32$) variants vastly beat the broken forcing trial ($0.35$), so the
ODE no longer collapses.

\subsection{Why physics does not help: explainability (H5)}\label{ssec:xai}
Two independent attribution methods \textbf{agree strongly} (Pearson $r=0.917$ across
feature groups; Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b), which is our robustness claim rather than any single
saliency map. Both find the forecast is driven by \textbf{pollutant lags and calendar}
features, while meteorology, photochemical and satellite inputs contribute little
(Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b). This directly explains H2: predictability lives in the
\othree/diurnal signal the network already learns from the lags, whereas the physics
coupling chiefly concerns the noisy, traffic-driven \nox\ component. Station permutation is
negligible ($\Delta$pinball $+0.0013$), and the day/night balance of attribution is nearly
flat (\nox-side day $0.364$ / night $0.387$), consistent with a learned map dominated by
recent concentrations.

\subsection{Spatial transfer (H4)}\label{ssec:transfer}
A station-agnostic QR-PINN generalises to held-out stations with a small, quantified
penalty: LOSO pinball $0.2277\pm0.0655$ vs.\ in-distribution $0.2024$, a transfer gap of
$+0.0253$. Per-station error tracks data volume and cleanliness (best at the large, clean
Barishal/Narayanganj folds, worst at the tiny BARC and Gazipur folds), supporting the
choice of \emph{global}, rather than regime-clustered, physics for transferability.
Standard spatial cross-validation caveats apply~\cite{roberts2017}.

\subsection{Interpretability is the physics payoff}
Although it does not sharpen accuracy, the embedded physics yields an interpretable,
\emph{physically ordered} parameterisation: learned photolysis ($0.352$) $\gg$ titration
($0.057$); a positive Arrhenius term; deposition ordered $\mathrm{NO}>\notwo>\othree$
($0.034/0.027/0.021$); and per-station emission scales that recover real city contrasts
(industrial Narayanganj high, residential peripheries low). The hybrid forecast tracks the
physics rollout (correlation $0.749$). One station's emission scale is an implausible
sparse-data artefact and is excluded. This positions the QR-PINN as a
\emph{physics-guided, interpretable} probabilistic forecaster rather than a
physics-accuracy mechanism.

%================================================================== CONCLUSION
\section{Conclusion}
We presented a quantile physics-informed neural network that forecasts the full 24\,h
predictive distribution of a balanced combined \nox--\othree\ hazard over Bangladesh. The
forecaster is well calibrated (CQR near-nominal across levels and lead time), transfers
across unseen stations with a bounded penalty, and is explainable. We tested whether
embedding \nox--\othree\ photochemistry improves accuracy and found, across three
independent experiments, that it does not on this \othree-led index; explainable AI
($r=0.917$ method agreement) shows the skill resides in pollutant-lag and calendar
features. We report this negative result transparently and reposition the physics as a
source of interpretability. Future work targets adaptive conformal inference for
non-exchangeable coverage~\cite{gibbs2021}, dropping the perfect-meteorology forcing
assumption, and explicit emission inventories.
